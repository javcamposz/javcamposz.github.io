# Site Impact Plan

## Objective

Make the site establish Javier as a board-ready AI and technology executive who can:

1. Lead an enterprise through AI and agentic transformation.
2. Strengthen a board's judgement on AI strategy, governance, investment, and risk.
3. Advise organisations on turning AI ambition into operating capability.

Until the October 2026 application decision, the immediate audience is the Future States hackathon selection team. The durable primary audience remains potential employers and executive-search decision-makers. Board and advisory opportunities are the second audience. AI consulting is the third.

Frontier AI safety research remains a major differentiator, but it should function as evidence of technical depth and judgement rather than as an equal commercial pathway.

The first ten seconds should answer four questions:

- Who is Javier?
- What senior mandate is he equipped to take on?
- What evidence supports that claim?
- What should the visitor do next?

## September 2026 Priority: No. 10 Innovation Hackathon

The immediate application objective is to demonstrate fit for [Future States: Hackathon at No. 10](https://luma.com/9vr0zktj) on 5-6 October 2026, while preserving the site's longer-term value for executive, board, and consulting opportunities. The call seeks engineers, researchers, and other builders who can make useful things around real state problems. The selection signal is therefore practical public-interest innovation, not only seniority, research authority, or general enthusiasm for AI.

This changes the near-term hierarchy, but not Javier's underlying positioning. The site should present Javier as a **builder-operator for high-consequence institutions**: someone who can frame a public problem, build at speed, test failure modes, work across disciplines, and translate a prototype into an accountable operating model.

### Selection signals the site should establish

1. **Public-purpose motivation:** interest in improving real institutions and citizen outcomes, not technology for its own sake.
2. **Hands-on building:** current, runnable software and evidence of shipping, not only executive sponsorship or thought leadership.
3. **Problem framing:** ability to move from an ambiguous service problem to a bounded intervention and measurable outcome.
4. **Safe delivery:** evaluations, human escalation, privacy, and operational controls treated as part of the build.
5. **Cross-functional range:** credibility with engineers, researchers, operational leaders, policymakers, and senior decision-makers.
6. **Pace with judgement:** the ability to prototype quickly without confusing a demonstration with production readiness.

### Strategic constraint

Do not turn the homepage into a temporary application campaign or state that Javier is applying. The public-service emphasis should be credible and durable for employers and boards. A CEO or chair should still recognise a group-level AI and technology leader; a hackathon reviewer should additionally recognise someone who builds and can contribute immediately.

## What To Learn From Eleanor Drage's Site

[Eleanor Drage's site](https://www.eleanordrage.com/) is effective because it behaves as a clear professional portal rather than a chronological CV.

### Patterns worth borrowing

- **Immediate category clarity:** her hero states speaker, broadcaster, and writer before presenting detail.
- **Strong personal signal:** name, photography, and subject area are visible immediately.
- **Simple navigation by visitor intent:** About, Media, Writing, Speaking, Testimonials.
- **Real-world imagery:** photography demonstrates the work rather than decorating the page.
- **Repeated third-party proof:** named organisations, media appearances, publications, and testimonials reinforce credibility.
- **Dedicated depth pages:** the homepage introduces each capability and lets visitors self-select.
- **One obvious conversion:** "Book Eleanor" matches the site's commercial purpose.

These patterns are visible across her [homepage](https://www.eleanordrage.com/), [about page](https://www.eleanordrage.com/about-eleanor), [writing archive](https://www.eleanordrage.com/writing), and [testimonials](https://www.eleanordrage.com/testimonials).

### What not to copy

Her site is designed to turn an event organiser into a speaking enquiry. Javier's site needs to turn a CEO, board member, executive-search partner, or transformation leader into a high-trust conversation.

Therefore:

- Replace speaking topics with executive mandates and business outcomes.
- Replace audience testimonials with CEO, board, peer, and client validation.
- Use speaking photography as supporting credibility, not the dominant proof.
- Prioritise operating impact before books, posts, events, and media.
- Use fewer large image galleries and more evidence-rich case summaries.
- Keep Safety AI and AI Engineering as distinctive proof of current expertise.

## Proof Model

Credentials alone do not establish impact. The site should deliberately combine four kinds of proof.

### Authority proof

- Group-level executive roles.
- Books and peer-reviewed papers.
- Research and teaching affiliations.
- Regulatory or institutional advisory experience.

### Operating proof

- Scale of teams and functions led.
- Platforms, products, and operating models built.
- Transformation mandates owned.
- Quantified outcomes where public and defensible.

### Independent proof

- Named testimonials.
- Published interviews and media.
- Conference invitations.
- External paper review or institutional recognition.

### Current-work proof

- Safety AI.
- AI Engineering.
- Recent essays.
- Current conference and research activity.

The existing site is strongest in authority and current-work proof. The largest content gap is operating proof.

Borrow Eleanor Drage's confidence and humanity, but adapt it to an executive audience.

- Warm neutral base with teal, red, blue, and restrained gold accents.
- Real photography showing leadership, board-level discussion, teaching, and technical work.

1. Executive portrait with direct eye contact.
2. Working session with senior leaders or a board-level audience.
3. Leadership or team setting.
4. Teaching and research context.

- Use full-width bands for positioning, proof, and contact.

- Keep CTAs explicit and role-specific.
- Make mobile navigation available; the current homepage hides it.
