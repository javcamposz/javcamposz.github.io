RSI Eval Lab: evaluating a system that can change itself.

Research prototype · synthetic traces · Python

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) makes evaluation a moving target: the solver, its scaffolding, and potentially the measurement process can change between generations. My RSI Eval Lab asks whether an apparent capability gain survives scrutiny of the controls and evidence behind it.

I designed and implemented a deterministic auditor of recorded lineages, with example traces, tests, an optional Inspect integration, and a research agenda. It can be run locally without a model or API key.

  1. Integrity of the evidence. Evaluator and monitor anchors are supplied outside the trace. Hash chains detect edits; a separate HMAC seal protects against recomputing the record when the candidate cannot access the key.
  2. Controls across generations. Declared invariants track whether requirements such as full action logging survive modification, including breaches that later appear repaired.
  3. What a score means. Challenge levels, public versus held-out scores, cost, and behaviour near rollback thresholds help expose results that need further investigation.

Start with a contrast

Improving scores, weakening oversight.

The invariant-drift example records better scores while action logging degrades. Compare it with the safe trace after installing the project:

rsi-eval examples/safe_run.json \
  --anchors examples/anchors.json

rsi-eval examples/invariant_drift_run.json \
  --anchors examples/anchors.json

What this does not establish: these are synthetic records. A sealed trace cannot prove its observations were true. Gate-related patterns do not establish intent. The tool does not execute or contain a self-improving agent.

What Is Safety AI?

Safety AI is the field concerned with making advanced AI systems reliable, controllable, and beneficial as their capabilities increase. It spans technical work, governance, evaluation, assurance, and institutional design.

My Focus

Evals, loss of control, and assurance.

My public work asks how we know whether an advanced AI system is safe enough to rely on, especially when it may act autonomously, use tools, reason over long horizons, and operate inside complex organizations.
